doz 0.52.12

The workspace lifecycle: sleep, wake, finish, reopen, archive, restore and delete — each shows its plan first.

  • doz workspace sleep | wake | finish | reopen | archive | restore | delete W. Every one first shows what happens to

each agent (a micro VM hibernates; a session on this Mac ends with its conversation kept and resumes on wake; an agent elsewhere is left alone), what is kept and what goes, then asks: nothing for sleep and wake, yes/no for finish, the workspace's name typed for archive and delete. --dry-run shows the plan and does nothing; --yes skips the question. A verb acts only on the plan you saw: if something changed meanwhile, it is refused and nothing is done.

  • Finish stops everything and keeps the room writable; reopen and wake bring it back. Archive makes its rooms read-only

and retires its anonymous workers (bots keep their keys); restore brings it back as finished.

  • Delete works only on a finished or archived workspace and removes its record. Nothing on disk goes unless you say so:

--remove-sandboxes removes the sandboxes Dozable made for it (a template of each kept first), and --bin-folders moves its folders to the Bin — never deletes them, and never your home folder or one another workspace uses. Its rooms stay, archived, with their history.

  • The dashboard: each workspace's page has a button for its next step (Start, Sleep, Wake, Reopen, Restore) and the rest

in its ⋯ menu, each showing the same plan before it acts; Home folds asleep workspaces into "Asleep". Only from this Mac's own browser; another computer sees the command instead.
